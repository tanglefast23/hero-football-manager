Unresolved

The CanvasKit abort was observed, but its cause is not isolated. Existing recovery already warns that the last action may be saved. A new graphics or save-recovery change would be speculative. No decision needed; it needs crash diagnostics and a repeatable trigger.

What happened in the playtest

A strong club story, with an uneven difficulty curve

The game is most engaging when a purchase, a training plan, or one tactical change can turn a close season.

That happened often enough to make the climb work. D3’s rematches, D2’s cup progress, and the late D1 comeback against Silverbeck were the strongest stretches.

The weakest stretches were early cup routs and a final season that became too comfortable. My original training criticism missed an existing shortcut. The best next step is to fix trust and clarity, then test the top end of the growth curve.

I would not make the entire game harder. This run had a difficult opening, real rebuilding choices, and a satisfying ending. Those parts deserve protection.

Before reducing every scout, compare the best available role upgrade, the transfer fee, the signed wage, and the promises required. Keep rare exciting finds. Reduce routine bargain dominance if broader samples confirm it.

Licenses and promises need one coherent explanation

Three separate rules interacted: unlicensed heroes could not play; full capacity blocked available promised starters from awakening; and later seasons allowed at most one ordinary awakening. The SUPER percentage shown on a player is the drill’s 1.5× gain chance, not their awakening chance.

The license purchase was meaningful. Having to read code to understand whether it could unlock progress was not. Show the current block in the permit office and at contract signing. Keep the annual pacing rule visible without revealing every random roll.

Protect is not proven broken

The first D1 Yewtree match fell from 3–1 to 3–3 after a defensive retreat. That is a reason to investigate outlets and pressure, not proof that Protect causes late concessions. Other earlier wins used it successfully.

Do not buff Protect solely to validate my expectation that a lead should be safe. Compare similar matches and inspect the chances created by both teams.

What was fun, and what was not

The game’s strongest moments connected a player, a long-term decision, and a result I could recognize.

Keep these sources of satisfaction

  • Players with a history. Ace, Cal and Sam remained relevant through the climb. Cal’s academy origin mattered at the final.
  • Visible power identities. Phase Run, Blink Run, Shadow Mark, Super Strength and Ice Rink made heroes distinct from a higher overall number.
  • Close rivals. Lumen, Peregrine, Garnet and Tarn gave individual seasons a shape.
  • Useful rematches. A team that beat us could later be beaten through a better squad and plan.
  • Recoverable failure. Missing promotion, taking a loan, and losing a cup tie did not end the career.
  • The authored ending. The final screen acknowledged both trophies and the relationship with Ace. It gave the run a proper stopping point.

Reduce these sources of fatigue

  • Training correction. A confirmed batch already finishes when its presentation is dismissed. Cross-player setup is normal management, not a proven defect.
  • Uncertain clicks. A hidden presentation layer sometimes made the next action feel ignored.
  • Long hopeless matches. After a huge cup gap became clear, few useful decisions remained.
  • Invisible dependencies. Permits and promises affected awakening without a clear combined explanation.
  • Repeated low-value choices. A reserve’s personal training penalty often made the request an easy yes.
  • Late routine wins. The final cup was a reward sequence more than a competitive test.

Presentation changes I would make carefully

Keep a full first awakening and a clear first explanation of each power. Keep the existing drill skip, SUPER feedback, and injury checks. Any smaller repeat power presentation remains a design choice.

Do not solve control visibility by freezing every power. The better target is continuous access to pause and tactical actions, with smaller repeat overlays.

What I would fix first — and what I would leave alone

First

Restore trust in actions and lineups

Fix CanvasKit recovery, diagnose the browser crash, and make lineup repair role-safe. A player should never need to inspect a save or catch a forward silently assigned to defense.

Close academy flipping narrowly. Measure scout bargains, high-rating training, energy modes, cup margins and D1 resistance over multiple careers. Change the smallest mechanism supported by the results.

Keep

Keep
