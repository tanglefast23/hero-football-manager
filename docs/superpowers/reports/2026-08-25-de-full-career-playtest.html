True ending reached without a crash

Bramble beat Cinder Vikings 4–0 away to win D1. The club had already won the Hero Cup final 2–1 against Alder Vanguard. The German true ending rendered, completed, and returned cleanly to the title screen.

Main balance decision: lower the strongest D1 club

The strongest D1 club begins around 124 average starting strength, including its special players. The preferred next test is 120. D1 took three seasons even after efficient upgrades and training.

The final season felt earned. The problem is the wait before it. D1 demanded two runner-up seasons before optimized play finally produced a 17–1–0 title season.

Fix first

Balance

D1’s strongest club starts too high

The top D1 starting XI is about 124 average strength, including special players. Bramble finished second twice despite focused weekly drills, maxed facilities, repeated Green Bull sessions, and a legal four-hero lineup.

Next change: tune that complete starting XI to about 120. Keep its identity and special players. Test whether an efficient club can now win D1 in one or two seasons.

German copy

Fire risk repeats the percentage

One German fire event displayed 70%: 70 % Risiko. The repeated number makes the choice harder to scan.

Fix: keep one localized percentage and one risk label. Check the same composition in every language.

Input

Some taps need a retry

Training close buttons and navigation tabs occasionally ignored the first click. Retrying immediately worked. This did not block progress, but it made the interface feel less reliable.

Fix: trace the shared press path before changing individual screens. The likely win is one root fix, not many local retry handlers.

Data risk

Duplicate player names remain possible

The active squad can contain duplicate display names. This run did not reproduce the old license collision, but duplicate names still make reports, promises, and license choices ambiguous.

Fix: keep player IDs as the only identity key and prevent duplicate full names inside one active squad when possible.

Division 1 deep dive

Season 8

The first warning

Bramble went 12–3–3 with a +25 goal difference. Cinder finished 17–1–0 on 52 points with a +69 goal difference. Optimized play was competitive, but not close to the title pace.

Season 9

The painful near miss

Bramble improved to 15–2–1 and 47 points. A 0–2 final-day loss to Cinder decided the title by two points. This was dramatic, but it required another full season after an already elite campaign.

Season 10

The complete answer

Bramble finished 17–1–0, scored 53, conceded seven, and beat Cinder 4–0 away in the decider. The final season strongly rewarded the long upgrade plan.

Verdict

Excellent climax, one season too late

D1 should remain the hardest division. The goal is not to make it easy. The goal is to let excellent play challenge the title sooner. A four-point top-club reduction is a small first move.

Efficient strategy that worked

  1. Spend useful TP every week. Train primary role attributes first. Rotate to the next useful player when an attribute reaches 999.
  2. Upgrade income before luxury facilities. Build and max the Stadium Stands and Fan Shops, then finish the useful training buildings and all seven drills.
  3. Use Green Bull before drills. It consumes all current TP. Enter with a small TP bank and keep enough cash for wages, renewals, and the next planned action.
  4. Treat Green Bull as a late money sink. This run used it at $550,000, $575,000, $600,000, and $625,000. The rising price made each purchase a real choice.
  5. Keep a legal natural-role lineup. The successful D1 shape was 5–3–2 with deep counter play, four licensed heroes, and manual checks after awakenings.
  6. Use Quick Result for the full career. It made a ten-season balance test practical and did not require manual power input.

Fun, fairness, and pacing

Fun

Long-term investment is visible

Tier 5 drills, maxed facilities, and repeated Green Bull sessions changed the team in ways the player could see. The final 4–0 win at Cinder felt like a payoff, not a random gift.

Fun

License pressure creates choices

Awakened players sometimes forced a formation or permit decision. The game supplied valid answers: buy a permit, train role depth, or change the lineup. That is useful difficulty.

Not fun

D1 repeats the same rivalry too long

Two second-place finishes made the final climb longer than the rest of the career. The club was visibly improving, but the target still required almost perfect league form.

Fair

The wall has real solutions

D1 was not broken. Better drills, facilities, heroes, role depth, and Green Bull training all helped. The recommended 124-to-120 adjustment should shorten the wait, not remove these choices.

Recommended change order

  1. Lower the strongest D1 starting XI to about 120. Include every special player in that target.
  2. Run one fresh efficient career. Check whether D1 can now be won in one or two seasons without weakening the earlier divisions.
  3. Fix the shared German fire-risk composition. Verify the equivalent sentence in all seven languages.
  4. Trace ignored presses at the shared input layer. Fix the root cause only if it reproduces outside browser automation.
  5. Keep duplicate names on the regression list. Confirm licenses, promises, and reports always use immutable player IDs.

Scope: one seeded German desktop-web career using Quick Result. This is strong pacing and usability evidence, but not a multi-seed balance sample or physical-phone layout test.
