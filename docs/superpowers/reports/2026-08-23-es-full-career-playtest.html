Fix first

Critical

Duplicate names break Hero Licenses

A new midfielder named Cal Moss awakened. The game then treated the original elite forward Cal Moss as unlicensed and benched him. The license list showed only one Cal Moss. Buying a sixth permit and selecting that entry made the forward eligible again, but the lineup still needed a manual swap.

Fix: bind awakenings, licenses, promises, and lineup slots to immutable player IDs. Names must be display text only. Also prevent duplicate display names inside the active squad, or add a clear role suffix everywhere.

High

Automatic lineup still makes bad swaps

Formation and awakening flows sometimes inserted a forward in defense, kept licensed stars out, or left the best valid player on the bench. Manual swaps worked.

Fix: preserve existing starters when the formation still has their role. Remove only surplus starters, lowest overall first. Then fill empty roles with the best valid natural-role player.

High

Scouting hides the signing path

Exact reports now return, and reported players do appear in Deals. The report cards still only offer discard. A player can reasonably think the report produced no signing path.

Fix: add one clear Go to Deals action on each completed report. Keep the player highlighted after navigation.

High

Contract controls still use fixed $50 steps

The Season 9 negotiation used $50 buttons. The requested percentage behavior is not in this deployed build.

Fix: make each press about 5% of the original asking wage, rounded to a readable amount. Keep the step stable during the same negotiation.

Medium

Season story ignores the double

The final review correctly showed D1 champion and Cup champion, but named “Lo ha pedido por escrito” as the story of the year. A routine player request should not outrank an unbeaten league-and-cup double.

Medium

Stale request messages remain

Ty Brooks' transfer-request inbox item remained after the request had ended. Old messages should say that the issue is resolved, or stop looking actionable.

Why Season 9 worked

  • 4–4–2 kept natural roles and put both elite forwards on the field.
  • The team beat Sable 3–2 away, 2–0 at home, and 4–1 in the Cup semifinal.
  • Green Bull was used in a no-match week before the decisive Sable game.
  • The final starter average reached about 197, led by a 461-rated forward.
  • The final match was won 2–0 away after one safe stamina event and one Pace drill.

D1 balance verdict

Sable's +64 goal difference looked frightening, but the club was beatable head-to-head. One D1 learning season was enough. This is much healthier than a loop where the top club grows faster than the player can catch it. Keep monitoring several fresh runs, but do not weaken Sable from this run alone.

Optimal strategy learned

  • Spend useful TP every week. Level 5 drills cost 39 TP. The final setup produced 62 TP weekly, supporting a steady one-drill rhythm and periodic two-drill weeks.
  • Rotate training targets. Max primary role attributes first, then improve weak starters and stamina.
  • Build income first. Complete at least two Stadium Stands and two Fan Shops before miscellaneous specialist facilities.
  • Upgrade drills. Level 5 drills produced +20 base and materially shortened the D1 catch-up.
  • Use Green Bull selectively. It costs all current TP, reduces condition, and rises by $25,000 each use. Spend the prior week's TP first. A no-match week is ideal.
  • Do not hoard cash without a purpose. Reserve wages, renewals, and the next transfer. Spend the rest on useful facilities, drill upgrades, or a well-timed Green Bull session.
  • Adapt to awakenings. License pressure is a good strategic problem when valid solutions remain: buy a permit, change formation, or train role coverage.
  • Quick Result is efficient. It made the long career practical and still produced a tense D1 finish.

Fun, fairness, and friction

Fun

  • The D1 title race reached the final week.
  • Direct Sable matches felt important and winnable.
  • Awakenings forced formation and license decisions.
  • Green Bull timing created a real money-versus-condition choice.
  • Scouting and developing the new midfielder created a clear recovery path.
  • The true ending was warm, personal, and worth reaching.

Not fun

  • Fixing automatic lineup mistakes before important matches.
  • Trying to tell two same-name players apart.
  • Leaving a scout report to discover the signing action elsewhere.
  • Seeing a routine request outrank the unbeaten double in the season story.
  • Receiving only $25,000 for a Hero Cup title described as a major prize.
  • Money requests can sound beneficial while secretly applying harmful multipliers.

Gift balance to verify

The deployed player card showed a gift price of three weekly salaries for +5 morale, with three club gifts available per week. This is much higher than the earlier 25%-salary proposal, but repeated +5 clicks are still slow. Test a +20 morale gift with a larger, clearly shown price. Do not finalize the number from this one career alone.

Recommended change order

  1. Fix all player identity flows to use immutable IDs. Add a regression test with two players sharing a name.
  2. Fix automatic lineup preservation and natural-role selection.
  3. Add a direct scout-report-to-Deals action.
  4. Replace fixed $50 contract steps with stable percentage steps.
  5. Make league-and-cup achievements outrank routine events in season-story selection.
  6. Resolve stale inbox actions and label their completed state.
  7. Remove remaining English fallback labels and repair Spanish accessibility accents.
  8. Clarify immediate Hero License activation and Cup prize expectations.

Overall: the career is completable, D1 now rewards optimized play, and the true ending works. The most dangerous remaining problem is player identity corruption when two active players share a name.
