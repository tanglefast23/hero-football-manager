True ending reached without a crash

Bramble finished D1 unbeaten, won the Hero Cup final 2–1 away at Bracken Rockets, completed every French true-ending beat, showed the creator message, and returned cleanly to the title screen.

Main balance finding: D1 changed too sharply between seasons

The first D1 season produced only five league goals and ninth place. The same career then went 17–1–0 and scored 45. The new 120-strength target is beatable, but the year-to-year swing needs investigation.

D1 was not too easy after the strength reduction. It was brutally hard once, then dominant one season later. That is a progression-cliff signal, not a reason to lower the top club again immediately.

Fix first

Balance

The D1 scoring swing is too large

Season 8 ended 3–7–8 with five goals scored. Season 9 ended 17–1–0 with 45. Focused Tier 5 drills and repeated Green Bull use were effective, but a nine-times scoring jump between adjacent seasons is too abrupt.

Next check: compare the Season 8 and Season 9 starting XI, opponent growth, formation, and Quick Result scoring inputs before changing D1 strength again.

French copy

Finance reports still leak English

French finance screens displayed Prime match Current Sponsor 1. The same dynamic fallback appeared in Season 8 and more than once in Season 9.

Fix: localize the shared generated sponsor label, then test the equivalent finance row in all seven languages.

Inbox

One request can count as two items

In Week 29, Home said 2 à traiter. It showed a “still waiting” card and a new locker-room card, but the Requests tab contained only Gio Stone’s single active request.

Fix: count unique active request IDs. A reminder can replace the original card, but it must not increase the badge.

Input

Some controls need an immediate retry

A few training close buttons and navigation tabs ignored the first click. The same click worked immediately on retry. This was not a progression blocker, but it reduced trust in the interface.

Fix: reproduce at the shared press layer before adding any screen-specific retry behavior.

Quick Result

Awakenings interrupt the fast path

Quick Result still opened an interactive awakening presentation. The match itself was quick, but the player had to tap through a live-style tutorial before reaching the report.

Fix: keep the reveal, but allow a single skip to move directly to the match report after the first viewed example.

Hero License

Free capacity still needs clearer awakening copy

Mae Thorn awakened while one of four permits was free, but the player still needed manual licensing. Dara Gray later awakened at 4/4 and correctly remained pending without damaging the lineup.

Fix: state clearly whether awakenings ever assign a free permit automatically. Current behavior is safe but unclear.

Division 1 deep dive

Season 8

The attack disappeared

Bramble scored only five times in 18 matches. The team still defended well enough to draw seven, but the forward strength did not translate into Quick Result goals. This season felt overly suppressed.

Season 9

The optimized loop took over

Bramble won 17 matches, drew one, and finished 22 points clear. The club scored 45, conceded five, and ended with a 6–0 away win. Gio Stone won Player of the Season, Ari Ward won Young Player, and Ivo Oak won Hero of the Season.

120 target

Do not lower D1 again yet

This preview included the reduced special-player tuning intended to bring the strongest full D1 team near 120. It still took two D1 seasons. One run supports keeping 120 for another test.

Investigation

Measure the progression cliff

Compare exact XI strength, role suitability, training multipliers, Green Bull uses, and opponent growth at both D1 season starts. The answer may be progression timing rather than opponent strength.

Efficient strategy that worked

  1. Spend useful TP every week. Focus on primary role attributes. Luc Moreau reached 999 Pace and Shooting; Tavi Reed reached 999 Defense; Sam Mitts reached 999 Reflexes.
  2. Rotate when a key attribute is capped. Later work moved into Gio Stone’s Shooting and Technique, Ivo Oak’s Technique, and Ari Ward’s Passing.
  3. Build the commercial base first. Three Level 3 Stadium Stands and three Level 3 Club Shops funded later facilities, salaries, gifts, and Green Bull.
  4. Upgrade the useful training chain. Most useful facilities reached Level 3. Six drills reached Tier 5; Reflexes remained Tier 4 because the starting goalkeeper was already capped.
  5. Use Green Bull before individual drills. It consumes all current TP. Spend ordinary TP first in other weeks, then enter a planned Green Bull week with enough TP and cash to justify it.
  6. Keep the natural 4–3–3 intact. The final lineup used natural roles, four licensed heroes, and no forced out-of-role player.
  7. Protect key weeks from player absences. Refuse two-week trips and filming requests near the Cup. Accept harmless training penalties when a capped player makes the downside irrelevant.
  8. Use Quick Result for the full career. It kept the nine-season test practical. Manual powers were not required.

Fun, fairness, and pacing

Fun

The long upgrade plan pays off

The final 6–0 league win and late Cup victories felt earned. Drill tiers, buildings, gifts, licensing, and Green Bull each had a clear job in the winning club.

Fair

Awakenings create solvable pressure

A new hero can make a formation harder to field. The player can buy a permit, train role depth, or change shape. That is useful difficulty when the license state is explained clearly.

Not fun

Season 8 offered too little attacking feedback

Five goals in 18 matches made upgrades feel invisible. Seven draws also created repetition: the team survived but rarely produced a satisfying payoff.

Pacing

Nine seasons is acceptable, but near the limit

Each earlier division needed at most two seasons. D1 also took two. The full arc stayed readable, though another failed D1 season would have made the ending feel late.

Recommended change order

  1. Investigate the D1 progression cliff. Compare the exact Season 8 and Season 9 inputs before changing opponent strength.
  2. Keep the strongest D1 club near 120 for now. Run one more efficient career before making another balance change.
  3. Fix the dynamic sponsor fallback. Verify the finance row in all seven languages.
  4. Deduplicate the request badge. One active request must count as one item even when it also has a reminder.
  5. Clarify awakening license behavior. Tell the player when a permit is free, pending, or requires manual assignment.
  6. Trace ignored presses once. Fix a shared input cause only if it reproduces outside browser automation.
  7. Make post-match awakenings one-tap skippable. Keep the first reveal, then preserve the Quick Result promise.

Scope: one French desktop-web career on the preview containing the adjusted D1 special-player tuning. All matches used Quick Result. This is strong pacing and localization evidence, but it is not a multi-seed balance sample or a physical-phone layout test.
